The previous walkthrough scored these two tracks head-to-head across 13 steps and four axes, as though they were two ways of doing one job. They are not. One writes a project design document from a form. The other rebuilds a package around a project design document that already exists. Scoring them against each other step by step produces a number that means nothing, which is why it read as confusing rather than clarifying.

Everything below compares them on what they demand from you and what they hand back — not on who wins.

Q5

Which one do I reach for, and when?

Lane A — Repo pipeline

Reach for it when the document does not exist yet and the numbers have to be defended.

  • A new project with no registered PDD to start from.
  • Numbers you must show your working for — it computes them, then cross-checks them across sections and blocks the export when they disagree.
  • Repeat projects in one family, where filling the form once pays off across many runs.
  • Not for: a project whose PDD already exists and only needs re-issuing.

Lane B — Portable workspace

Reach for it when the document exists and the job is to re-issue it without losing the evidence.

  • A registered project moving to a newer Verra template.
  • Evidence-heavy submissions where every source page and workbook must travel with the document.
  • Mixed piles of input files nobody has time to transcribe into a form.
  • Not for: a project with no PDD, or one whose emissions numbers need re-deriving.

So whatFor Inegol specifically — a registered project with a 139-page PDD already on file — Lane B matches the job. Lane A’s value on Inegol is as a checker: it independently computed 893,441 tCO₂e over seven years, which is a number worth putting beside the registered one. The useful question was never which lane wins. It is whether Lane A’s arithmetic agrees with the document Lane B is preserving.
